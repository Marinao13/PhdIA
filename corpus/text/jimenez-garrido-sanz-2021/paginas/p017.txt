Strongly regular sequences and regular variation
In this section we will recall the notion of regular variation introduced in 1930 by J. Kara-
mata ([20, 21]), although previous, partial treatments may be found in the works of E.
Landau[25] and G. Valiron[57]. The proof of most of the forthcoming results can be found
in the books of E. Seneta[52] and N. H. Bingham, C. M. Goldie, and J. L. Teugels [7]. We will
use the theory of regular variation to give a characterization of strongly regular sequences,
and we will also show that the growth index γ(M) deﬁned and studied by V. Thilliez [55]
and the order of quasianalyticity ω(M) are the same whenever dM is a proximate order.

There is an immediate relation between regular variation and proximate orders in our
context: dM is a proximate order if, and only if, the function M(t) is regularly varying
(see [7, Subsection 7.4.1]).
