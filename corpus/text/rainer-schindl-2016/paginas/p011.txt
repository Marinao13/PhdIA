4 Proof of Theorem 6: the Beurling case
The structure of the proof of the equivalence of the six items in Theorem 6 is again represented
by the diagram in (3.1), where now:
(6′) If f ∈E(M)(R) and f (0) ̸= 0 then 1/f is E(M) on its domain of deﬁnition.
4.1 The equivalences (1) ⇔(2) ⇔(3)
Lemma 1 implies (1) ⇔(2), since (MH) follows from (M(Cω)). The equivalence of (2) and
(3) was shown in [13, 4.11].
4.2 The cycle (1) ⇒(4) ⇒(6′) ⇒(1)
That (4) implies (6′) follows in the same way as in the Roumieu case, see Subsect. 3.2. The
implication (6′) ⇒(1) is a consequence of the following lemma.
