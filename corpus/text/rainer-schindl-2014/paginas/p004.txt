Let F and G denote classes of mappings. We write F ⊆G if F(U, Rm) ⊆
G(U, Rm) for all open subsets U ⊆Rn and all n, m ∈N>0. We say that
F is stable under composition if g ∈F(U, V ) and f ∈F(V, W) implies
f ◦g ∈F(U, W), for all open subsets U ⊆Rp, V ⊆Rq, W ⊆Rr, and all
p, q, r ∈N>0. A class F is called holomorphically closed if f ◦g ∈F(U, C)
for each g ∈F(U) = F(U, R) and each f which is holomorphic in a complex
neighborhood of the range of g, and F is inverse closed if 1/f ∈F(U)
for each non-vanishing f ∈F(U). That F is derivation closed means that
f ∈F(U) implies ∂if ∈F(U) for all open U ⊆Rn, n ∈N>0, and 1 ≤i ≤n.
A class F of smooth mappings is quasianalytic if for each open connected
U ⊆Rn and each x ∈U the Borel mapping F(U) ∋f 7→(∂αf(x))α is
injective.
