8.2
More on Transformation to Rational Form
In Theorem 11 (p. 52) we showed that a formal system can be transformed
into a rational one – meaning a system with a coeﬃcient matrix being a ra-
tional function with poles at the origin and inﬁnity only. Here we shall prove
that, starting with a system (3.3) whose coeﬃcient matrix is r-summable,
then so is the transformation obtained in the said theorem. Indeed, much
more can be said:
Proposition 19 For some d ∈R, let zx′ = ˆA(z) x and z˜x′ = ˆB(z) ˜x be
two formal system of Poincar´e rank r ≥1 whose coeﬃcient matrices are
r-summable in some direction d, and let ˆT(z) be a formal meromorphic
transformation of Gevrey order s = 1/r satisfying (3.5). Furthermore, as-
sume that −rd ̸= arg(µ −˜µ) mod 2π, for any two distinct eigenvalues µ, ˜µ
of the leading term A0. Then ˆT(z) is also r-summable in direction d.
Proof: Using Exercise 4 on p. 41, applied to the transpose of ˆT(z), we factor
ˆT(z) = ˆT0(z) T(z) with a terminating meromorphic transformation T(z)
and a formal analytic transformation ˆT0(z) of Gevrey order s and leading
term I. Setting ˆB0(z) = (T(z) ˆB(z) + zT ′(z))T −1(z), we see that ˆB0(z)
is also r-summable in direction d, while ˆT(z) is r-summable in direction
d if and only if the same holds for ˆT0(z). This shows that in the proof
we may without loss of generality assume that ˆT(z) is a formal analytic
transformation with constant term I, and then the leading term of ˆB(z) is
equal to A0, and its Poincar´e rank is again equal to r.
